      Formation checklist

        Licensing gates

        Security installs: Texas Occupations Code Ch. 1702. Installing or servicing alarm, video-surveillance, or electronic access-control systems for hire needs a DPS Class B security contractor license with a qualified manager, plus individual installer registration (fingerprints, background check). Operating without one is a Class A misdemeanor. IT-side identity work needs no license.

        Home PM: no Texas general-contractor license; a PM coordinating licensed trades needs none. Electrical, plumbing, HVAC must be state-licensed trades; some cities require contractor registration. Buying/selling/leasing for others needs a TREC license; managing a renovation does not.

        Braces: Texas sales-tax permit for retail sales; no medical license for non-custom, over-the-counter braces.

        The truck-sharing agreement · terms

        Title and loanTitled to Jeremiah, financed personally. The LLC does not own the truck; it has a documented right to use it.
        Cost poolLoan payment, insurance, registration ($200 EV fee included), charging, tires, maintenance, washes. Tracked monthly in one sheet the logger app feeds.
        Split ruleCosts allocated each month by logged miles. Business miles reimbursed to Jeremiah at the IRS rate, or the LLC pays its actual-cost share, whichever method the CPA chose for the year.
        Uber incomeGoes to the household; reported on the driver's Schedule C unless the CPA routes it through the LLC.
        Named driversJeremiah, Jesica, Cooper, Myles, all on the policy. Every trip logged with driver and purpose. Unlogged trips billed to the driver at the standard rate.
        Cooper's path to ownershipHe pays his share of the pool from day one; refinances jointly once he has 6–12 months of pay stubs, alone after 12–24 months of on-time payments. Buyout = down payment + principal paid − his contributions.
        DamageDeductible on any at-fault incident is paid by whoever was driving.
        ReviewQuarterly, alongside the scorecard: business-use %, net Uber offset, whether the truck still pays for itself.

        Assumes the truck is over 6,000 lb GVWR (all full-size EV pickups are), the LLC is a real business with the income entered above, and business miles are logged. Self-employment tax saving applies only up to the LLC's net income; deductions beyond that offset W-2 income at the bracket rate. No federal EV purchase credit exists for vehicles acquired after Sept 30, 2025. Recapture applies if business use drops to 50% or below or the truck is sold early. Planning arithmetic, not tax advice; the CPA picks the method in year one and it generally can't be switched to standard mileage afterward.

        If it's an actual emergency

        Call 911 or go to the nearest ER. Don't stop to check anything on this page.

        Trouble breathing, a bad head injury, heavy bleeding, chest pain, a spreading allergic reaction, someone unconscious. Emergencies are covered at any hospital, in-network or not. Then call or text Dad; if you can't reach him, call Mom.

        1. Medical insurance is UnitedHealthcare; pharmacy is OptumRx. When a front desk asks "who's your insurance?", that's the answer.

        Words you'll hear at the front desk

        Copay
        A small fixed amount you pay at the visit. Insurance pays the rest. Different for a doctor, urgent care, ER, and prescriptions.
        Deductible
        What the family pays out of pocket each year before insurance pays the bigger share on big things like hospital stays or MRIs. Copay visits usually don't count against it.
        In-network / out-of-network
        In-network: the doctor has a contract with UHC, we pay the low price. Out-of-network: no contract, we pay most of it. Check before any non-emergency visit.
        Claim / EOB
        The bill the office sends the insurer. Weeks later an "Explanation of Benefits" arrives. It looks like a bill but it isn't one.
        Prior authorization
        Insurance saying "check with us before the expensive thing." The doctor's office handles the form.
        Guarantor
        The person responsible for the bill. That's Dad. Write his name and number, not yours.
        HSA / FSA
        Pre-tax money set aside for medical costs. If Dad hands you a card that looks like a debit card for a copay, that's this.
