Settlement now, bonus next

A twelve-month plan in two phases. The $12–15K from the medical claims builds the base and buys the car cleanly this fall. The Q1 bonus from Texas Mutual lands on that base and pushes the growth, venture, and business pieces that a five-figure settlement can't fund by itself.

Assumptions: no credit-card or other high-interest debt outstanding, the Fidelity 401(k) match is already being captured, the emergency fund is under three months of expenses, and the bonus is shown at a placeholder $5,000 gross (about $3,500 after withholding). Swap in the real bonus figure and everything in Phase 2 scales with it.

Reserve savings

$4,500

Open a high-yield savings account (top online banks are paying 4.00–4.21% APY; the national average is 0.38%) and this is the first dollar in. It is what keeps a car repair or a surprise EMS bill off a credit card, and it is the account the LLC seed and the breathing-room money also sit in until they're used, so the balance on day one is closer to $6,750 earning interest.

Vehicle down payment

$4,000

  • $4,000 down is 20% on a $20,000 used vehicle or 10% on a $40,000 new one. Stay in that range so the loan is never larger than the car is worth.

  • Don't raid the reserve for a bigger down payment. Against a 4.4% loan, cash earning 4% in savings is doing nearly the same job while staying available. Phase 2 has a slot for extra principal if you want it.

Roth IRA index funds

$2,000

Open a Roth IRA at Fidelity alongside the 401(k) and put this in a low-cost total-market or S&P 500 index fund. This is the long-horizon growth half of "savings"; the 2026 limit is $7,500 per person under 50, so there's $5,500 of room left for the bonus to fill.

On "heavy revenue gains": $6,500 in safe savings earns about $260 a year at today's rates, and nothing safe pays materially more. Anything advertising 8–10% on cash has strings or isn't cash. The return on this money comes from years in index funds, which is why the Roth exists in Phase 1 even at a small size.

LLC / business seed, parked

$1,500

Earmarked, sitting in the HYSA, not spent. A Texas LLC costs $300 to file and has no annual state fee (the franchise tax report owes nothing below about $2.5M revenue), so cost isn't the reason to wait; purpose is. Form it when there is a first product, customer, or contract in view, whether that's Cargill Techware Braces or something Jesica runs. Cooper is a minor, so the workable structure is Jesica as member and Cooper as a paid helper, which lets his earnings fund a Roth in his name. Don't form an LLC just to hold savings; it protects nothing at this size and forfeits the Roth's tax treatment.

Venture slice

$750

Real VC funds require accredited-investor status and five-figure minimums, so at this scale the options are Regulation Crowdfunding platforms (Wefunder, Republic, StartEngine, $100–$1,000 per company), a small-cap or thematic ETF, or your own business. Spread it across a few names, expect most to go to zero, and treat the slice as tuition. It grows in Phase 2.

Family breathing room

$750

Spent on purpose, no guilt. A plan with zero slack gets abandoned; this is what keeps the other 94% intact.

The 3x questionTurning ~$17,000 into $40,000

Settlement midpoint plus the placeholder net bonus is about $17,000. Tripling it means reaching roughly $40,000. There are four ways money triples, and they differ mostly in how long they take and what they cost you.

PathTime to 3xOddsWhat it costs
Index funds in the Roth, left alone11–16 yrsHighPatience. Rule of 114: at 7% average returns money triples in ~16 years, at 10% in ~11.
Adding cash flow every month2–4 yrsHighDiscipline. The bonus and the household surplus do the work; the market only helps.
Running the LLC as a real business1–2 yrsMediumLabor. Capital turns over several times a year, but Jesica and Cooper's hours are the input.
Speculation (options, crypto, single stocks, Reg CF)Months, or neverLowMost of the stake. Capped at 10% because the median outcome is a loss.
The plain version: no investment turns $17,000 into $40,000 in a year without a real chance of turning it into $5,000 instead. In a 6–12 month window, tripling comes from either adding money or running a business. The safe money compounds in the background and gets you the rest of the way over the following years.

Path 3 in numbers: the brace business as the accelerator

This is the only bucket where 3x inside twelve months is plausible, because you're converting hours into return rather than waiting on a rate. Illustrative, using the $1,500 seed plus the $700 bonus launch money:

  • $2,200 of inventory bought at wholesale, priced at roughly 2x cost (typical for durable medical goods sold direct).
  • Turn the inventory four times in a year and the seed produces about $17,600 in revenue and roughly $8,800 gross profit before fees, shipping, and the $300 filing.
  • Reinvest the first two turns' profit into inventory and the business alone can put $6,000–7,000 of new money into the household in year one, on top of the $17,000. Combined with $500/month of surplus, that lands within reach of $40,000 by roughly month 24.
  • The failure mode isn't losing the $2,200; it's inventory that doesn't move. Start with one or two SKUs that already have demand, not the full five-product line.

Path 4: the speculation slice, rules of engagement

  • Never more than 10% of total invested money, rebalanced down if it grows past that.
  • Only money already written off in the plan; a total loss changes nothing above.
  • If a position does 3x, sell two-thirds and move it to the Roth. The slice is a lottery ticket that funds the boring engine when it hits, not a strategy that gets bigger when it wins.

The free 3x most people skip

Every dollar of 401(k) contribution that the Texas Mutual match covers is an instant 50–100% return before the market does anything, and Roth growth is never taxed. If the match isn't fully captured yet, raising the deferral is a better use of the bonus than any investment on this page, and it's why the assumption at the top matters.

The vehicle, revisitedA truck through the LLC: what the tax benefit really is

The rules are generous right now, and a truck can be the smartest asset the business owns. But the deduction is earned by business use, not by the LLC's name on the title, and a new LLC changes how the loan gets priced. Here is how to make it work.

What the 2026 rules allow

Rule2026What it means for a truck
Bonus depreciation100%, permanent for property placed in service after Jan 19, 2025The business-use share of the truck's cost can be written off in year one, and this one is not limited to business income.
Section 179$2.56M limit; $32,000 first-year cap on SUVs 6,000–14,000 lb GVWRPickups with a 6-foot-plus bed are exempt from the SUV cap. 179 is limited to the business's net income; excess carries forward.
Weight thresholdOver 6,000 lb GVWREscapes the "luxury auto" depreciation caps that hit cars and light SUVs. Most half-ton pickups (F-150, Silverado 1500, Ram 1500, Tundra) qualify on GVWR; check the door-jamb sticker.
Business useMust exceed 50%, documentedDeduction equals cost × business %. A mileage log is not optional; if use later drops to 50% or below, the deduction is recaptured.
Standard mileage (alternative)72.5¢/mile Jan–Jun, 76¢/mile Jul–Dec 2026Skip depreciation entirely and deduct per business mile. Simpler, and better for a truck that mostly drives the family.

What it's worth, honestly

  • A $40,000 truck at 60% business use gives a $24,000 first-year deduction under bonus depreciation. In the 22% bracket that's about $5,300 of federal tax back, more if it also reduces self-employment tax. At 100% business use the number roughly doubles. Real money, but it's a discount on the truck, not a way to pay for it.
  • Taking 100% in year one means nothing to deduct in years two through five, and if you sell or trade the truck early, the gain is taxed as ordinary income (recapture). The deduction is timing, not free money.
  • The IRS looks hardest at a brand-new business whose first big expense is a vehicle. The brace business needs real sales, its own bank account, and a log showing the truck hauling inventory to markets, clinics, or shipping drops. Deliveries, trade shows, and supplier runs are business miles; commuting to Texas Mutual is not.
  • Texas has no state income tax, so the deduction is federal only. Sales tax (6.25% + local) is owed on the purchase either way.

The sequencing that gets both the rate and the deduction

  • Buy in your name, not the LLC's, for the loan. A months-old LLC has no credit file. A commercial auto loan will be personally guaranteed anyway and priced 1–3 points higher than the super-prime 4.41% you can get personally. The tax rules don't require the LLC to hold title; a sole proprietor's or single-member LLC's vehicle used in the business qualifies the same way.
  • Choose the method by how the truck will actually be used. If it's the family's daily driver that also does business runs, take standard mileage and keep the plan's $4,000-down, 60-month structure as written. If it will be more than 50% business, take bonus depreciation on the business share and keep a log from day one; you must pick a method in year one and generally can't switch to standard mileage later.
  • Time the purchase to the business's first real year. Placed in service in December 2026 with no 2026 sales looks like a hobby. Placed in service in early 2027 with inventory moving and the LLC filed in January is a business asset. That means the Phase 1 vehicle purchase either waits until the LLC decision in December, or Phase 1 buys a modest personal vehicle now and the truck becomes a Phase 2 / year-two purchase once the business has revenue to justify it.
  • Insurance follows use. Once the truck carries inventory for sale, the personal auto policy may exclude the loss. A business-use endorsement is usually cheaper than a full commercial policy and is what the log will point to anyway.
  • Down payment stays at $4,000 to $6,000. The deduction doesn't change the cash-flow argument from Phase 1: at 4.4% financing against 4% savings, extra cash down buys little. Let the tax refund from the first business year go to the reserve or to extra principal.
Bottom line: the LLC is a liability shield, not a tax benefit; the truck deduction belongs to whoever runs the business, LLC or not. Make the brace business real first, buy the truck on your own excellent credit, log every business mile, and let a CPA pick between mileage and depreciation the first year it's on the return. Done in that order, the truck is a legitimately subsidized purchase. Done in reverse, it's a personal vehicle with an audit flag on it.
