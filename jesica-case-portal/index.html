On September 1 you filed the Chapter 13 petition and, the same day, a Motion to Voluntarily Dismiss (Document 6). The docket shows no order on that motion yet. Until a judge signs a dismissal order, the case is open: the automatic stay is protecting you from collection, and the court is treating the case as if it will go forward — that's why the deficiency order, the trustee's notice, and the meeting date all arrived anyway.

Creditors had until about September 22 to object to your motion. If nobody objects, the court can rule without a hearing. Watch the docket (or call the clerk) for an Order Dismissing Case.

If you still want the case dismissed

You generally don't need to file anything else — a Chapter 13 debtor has a right to dismiss her own case (11 U.S.C. §1307(b)) as long as the case was never converted from another chapter. The order should come in due course.

Even so, the court has a second path to dismissal running in parallel: the Order: Possible Future Dismissal (Document 8) says the case is dismissed automatically on about October 16 if the missing schedules aren't filed. Either path ends in the same place.

What changes when the order is signed: the automatic stay ends, and Freedom Mortgage (and anyone else) can resume normal collection, including foreclosure steps. The $313 filing fee is not refunded.

If you've changed your mind and want to keep the case

Then time matters a great deal. You would need to withdraw the motion to dismiss and file everything listed in Document 8 — the schedules (A/B through J), Statement of Financial Affairs, Form 122C-1, pay stubs or the no-pay-advice declaration — plus a Chapter 13 plan and a payment order, and start paying the trustee. The first trustee payment was due by October 1.

Doing this pro se is hard. Before deciding, talk to a bankruptcy attorney — see People & contacts for free and low-cost options in Galveston County.

          What the words mean

          Terms that appear in your documents, explained.

          Chapter 13
          A repayment plan bankruptcy for people with regular income. Debts are paid over 3–5 years through a trustee; you keep your property while the plan runs. Compare Chapter 7, which sells non-exempt property and wipes out debts faster.
          Petition
          The form that starts the case (Document 1). The moment it is stamped, the automatic stay begins and you are a "debtor."
          Pro se
          Representing yourself, without a lawyer. The docket shows you as "represented by Jesica Ann Green, PRO SE."
          Automatic stay
          An immediate legal freeze on most collection: no calls, letters, lawsuits, garnishments, repossessions or foreclosure sales while the case is open. It ends when the case is dismissed or closed.
          Voluntary dismissal (§1307(b))
          A Chapter 13 debtor's right to end her own case by asking the court. Your Document 6 is that request. Dismissal is different from discharge — a dismissed case wipes out nothing; the debts remain as they were.
          Discharge
          The court order that legally erases debts covered by the plan. In Chapter 13 it comes only after all plan payments are made. It will not happen in a dismissed case.
          Schedules (A/B, C, D, E/F, G, H, I, J)
          The detailed lists the court requires: what you own (A/B), what you claim as exempt (C), who holds liens on your property (D), other creditors (E/F), leases and contracts (G), co-debtors (H), income (I) and expenses (J). Document 8 says none were filed.
          Statement of Financial Affairs (Form 107)
          A questionnaire about your finances over the last few years — income, payments, lawsuits, transfers.
          Form 122C-1
          The "means test" for Chapter 13: compares your income to the Texas median to set how long the plan must run (3 or 5 years).
          Payment advices
          Pay stubs from the 60 days before filing. If you had no employer income, you sign the one-paragraph declaration attached to Document 8 instead.
          §521(i) automatic dismissal
          A rule that a case is dismissed on its own on the 46th day if the required schedules and statements still aren't filed. For a September 1 petition that is about October 16, 2026.
          Chapter 13 plan
          Your proposal for how much you'll pay the trustee each month and how that money is split among creditors. The court must approve ("confirm") it at a confirmation hearing.
          Confirmation hearing
          The court date where the judge decides whether to approve the plan. Set for December 3, 2026 at 9:00 AM in Galveston — only relevant if the case continues and a plan is filed.
          Meeting of creditors (§341 meeting)
          A short sworn Q&A run by the trustee, not the judge. Creditors may attend but rarely do. Yours is set for October 19, 2026 at 10:00 AM by Zoom. You would need photo ID and proof of your Social Security number.
          Trustee
          Tiffany Castro — the person appointed to collect plan payments, review your paperwork, and pay creditors. Not your lawyer and not the judge.
          U.S. Trustee
          A Justice Department office that oversees bankruptcy cases and trustees. Different from the Chapter 13 trustee.
          Proof of claim
          A creditor's form saying how much it is owed and why. TWC filed Claim 2-1 for $366. Claim 1-1 exists on the register but hasn't been downloaded yet. The deadline for most creditors to file is November 10, 2026.
          Secured vs. unsecured
          Secured debt is backed by property the lender can take (your mortgage — the house). Unsecured debt has no collateral (the TWC overpayment). Priority unsecured debts, like recent taxes, get paid before general unsecured ones.
          Adequate protection
          Payments or conditions that protect a lender's collateral while the case is pending. Document 9's vehicle order sets automatic adequate-protection terms for any car loan — it's a standard form that goes in every case, whether or not you have a car loan.
          Wage order / EFT order / ACH order
          The way plan payments would reach the trustee: deducted from wages, or pulled electronically from your bank. One would have to be filed with the plan.
          BNC — Bankruptcy Noticing Center
          The mail service that sends court notices. Documents 10 and 11 are its certificates saying who was mailed what and when.
          Dischargeability complaint (§523)
          A lawsuit by a creditor arguing a particular debt should survive the bankruptcy (e.g., fraud). Deadline December 18, 2026. None filed.
          Debtor education / financial management course
          A second, post-filing class (different from the pre-filing credit counseling you completed Aug 28). Required before any discharge; due December 18, 2026 if the case continues.
          Homestead exemption
          Texas law protects your primary residence from most creditors in bankruptcy, regardless of value (with an acreage cap). The appraisal district record shows the homestead ("HS") exemption on 14 Prairie Oaks. This does not stop the mortgage lender, whose lien you agreed to.
          PACER
          The federal courts' online records system (pacer.uscourts.gov). Most documents cost $0.10 per page to view. The clerk's office can also print them for a fee.
