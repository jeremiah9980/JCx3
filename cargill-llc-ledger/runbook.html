Strategy in one screen

Cargill Consulting is a consulting shell: it engages small and medium businesses on a project basis. Cooper and Myles deliver access-control and security-system installs and small-business websites; Jesica runs Cargill Home (owner's-rep project management); Jeremiah is the senior engineer behind every proposal. The LLC is registered at Jesica's La Marque address, owned 51% by Jesica so it qualifies as woman-owned, and it earns its way in through certifications and local buyers rather than through incentives written for factories.

What pays, in order

  • Texas HUB certification + local vendor registrationsFree. Puts the LLC on the lists that La Marque, Texas City, Galveston County, College of the Mainland and TCISD are pushed to buy from — and small IT/security jobs under their bid thresholds can be awarded without a solicitation. The most reliable first-year revenue source on this page.
  • La Marque EDC Chamber Membership GrantA paid first year in the Texas City–La Marque Chamber: every clinic, realtor and shop owner who'd buy a camera install or a website is in that room. Take it the week the LLC exists.
  • The EDC as navigator, not funderTheir "grant or loan procurement assistance" means staff who help a local business find and apply for outside money — and who know which Enterprise Zone employers must hire zone residents. That's a placement channel for Cooper and Myles and a customer list.
  • WOSB (federal) + Roth IRAs + business creditFree credentials that compound: WOSB through SBA, earned income from LLC wages opening Roths at 22, a D-U-N-S file and net-30 tradelines building a Paydex 80 within a year.
  • DPS route via a licensed partnerIdentity/network work needs no license; camera and access installs do. Cooper and Myles register as installers under a licensed alarm contractor, bank the documented experience, and the LLC pursues its own Class B in year two.

What we're not chasing yet — and why

  • Texas Enterprise Zone ProgramA state sales-tax refund for a city-nominated "enterprise project": scarce nominations (105 statewide per biennium), capital investment from ~$40,000 up, full-time jobs at the county average wage, 25% zone-resident hires. Not a two-person LLC in year one. Revisit at a shop, a van and installers on payroll.
  • Tax abatements · IRBs · FreeportNeed taxable property, a facility, or inventory leaving the state. None apply to a home-based consultancy.
  • Texas Leverage Fund · Capital Fund · TMACMoney the city or EDC borrows for an employer's infrastructure, and a manufacturing extension program. Not for us.
  • Business Improvement / Sign fundsCurb-appeal grants for a storefront on a main thoroughfare. Worth knowing if Cargill Home ever takes a space; not worth chasing before.
  • Utility rate reductions · fee waivers · fast-trackNegotiated on industrial usage and real construction. File under "later".
The one structural decision that unlocks everything above: Jesica holds at least 51% of the membership interest and runs day-to-day operations. That is the test for Texas HUB (woman-owned, Texas resident, active in the business) and for federal WOSB. Jeremiah at 49% keeps his role as senior engineer and reviewer without touching eligibility. Cooper and Myles join later by a written path to membership. Decide this in RB-01 — before filing, not after.
